Answer Key
A. Read the Text

Text: Mia and Ben. No written answers – students read and notice like / doesn't like + the things each person prefers.

B. Answer the Questions
  • 1. She likes art, music and cats.
  • 2. She doesn't like spiders (or loud video games).
  • 3. He likes soccer, dogs and pizza.
  • 4. He doesn't like carrots (or waking up early).
  • 5. They are good friends and they always have fun together (even though they like different things).
  • 6. Yes, they are.
C. True or False
1. T
4. T
2. F (she doesn't like spiders)
5. F (they like different things)
3. T
6. T
D. Find the Expressions

Answers vary – one phrase copied from the text for each: 1. She likes art, music and cats. 2. He likes soccer, dogs and pizza. 3. She doesn't like spiders. 4. He doesn't like carrots.

E. Vocabulary in Context
1. art (painting)
4. carrots
2. spiders
5. early
3. soccer
F. Think About It / Your Turn

Personal answers. Students write at least two things they like and two they don't like, in full sentences (I like… / I don't like…).

Teaching Support
Teacher Tip
The text pairs every 'likes' with examples and every 'doesn't like' with examples. Before Exercise B, have students underline all the 'likes' phrases in one colour and all the 'doesn't like' phrases in another – the answers are then almost copied out.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ Mia like art.
✓ Mia likes art.
✗ She don't like spiders.
✓ She doesn't like spiders.
✗ Ben and Mia likes the same things.
✓ Ben and Mia like the same things.
✗ He doesn't likes carrots.
✓ He doesn't like carrots.
Extra Class Activity
Same or Different? (8 min)
Students write two things they like and two they don't like. In pairs they compare: 'I like pizza.' 'Me too!' or 'I don't.' They report one thing they have in common and one difference to the class using we / I.

Likes and Dislikes – Reading • A1
Learning Focus
Objective: Students will be able to read a short text about two people's preferences and answer comprehension questions in full sentences using likes / doesn't like.
Success Criteria: I can find what someone likes and doesn't like in a text. I can answer questions with 'He / She likes…' or 'He / She doesn't like…'. I can say if a sentence is true or false.
Prior Knowledge: like / likes / don't like / doesn't like (from the Grammar worksheet); food, animal and hobby vocabulary.
Lesson Plan (40–45 minutes)
Level: A1 Skill: Reading Topic: Preferences (like / don't like) in a short text (Mia and Ben) Exercises: 6 (A–F) Est. Time: 40–45 minutes
Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Two coloured pencils or highlighters Pens
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpAsk 2–3 students 'What do you like? What don't you like?' Write two answers on the board as 'She likes…' / 'He doesn't like…', underlining likes / doesn't like. Say: today we read about two people, Mia and Ben, and find what each one likes.
10 minPresentation (I Do)Read the Mia paragraph aloud while students follow with a pencil. Teach the strategy: (1) Exercise B questions follow the text order, so read paragraph by paragraph; (2) 'likes' is followed by what she likes, 'doesn't like' by what she doesn't – those are the answer phrases. Think aloud on question 1: 'It asks what Mia likes → paragraph 1 says “She likes art, music and cats” → full answer: She likes art, music and cats.' Repeat with a 'doesn't like' question. Note the answer keeps the -s ('She likes', not 'She like').
10 minGuided Practice (We Do)Exercise B questions 1–3 together: a student reads the question, the class says which paragraph and which sentence holds the answer, then everyone writes the full-sentence answer. Check the verb form (likes / doesn't like) on the board each time. Students finish B alone and compare in pairs.
10 minIndependent Practice (You Do)Students complete Exercise C (true/false – they must find and underline the proof sentence for each), Exercise D (copy one example phrase per pattern) and Exercise E (vocabulary from clues) alone.
6 minAssessmentReview Exercise C as a class: for each item a student reads the statement and the exact sentence in the text that makes it true or false. Students then complete the Exit Ticket individually; collect it.
5 minClosureStudents write their Exercise F answers (2 things they like, 2 they don't) and read one 'I like' and one 'I don't like' sentence to a partner, who says 'Me too' or 'I don't'.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Homework: write a short paragraph (4–5 sentences) about a friend's likes and dislikes, in the style of the Mia / Ben text.
Anticipated Problems & Solutions
Problem: Students answer with just a word ('art') instead of a full sentence.
Solution: Write the sentence frame 'She likes ____.' / 'He doesn't like ____.' on the board and require every Exercise B answer to use it.
Problem: Students drop the -s ('Mia like art') or add -s after doesn't ('doesn't likes').
Solution: Point back to the Grammar worksheet rule; check the verb form on the board for the first two answers.
For Extra Support For Exercise B, give weaker students the question and the matching sentence from the text side by side, so they only rewrite it as a full-sentence answer.
For an Extra Challenge Students write three more true/false statements about the text and swap with a partner.
Extension & Fast-Finisher Ideas
Write one thing Mia and Ben both probably like or dislike, and why. Find and copy two 'often' sentences from the text.
Language Focus & Board Notes
Grammar/Vocab: likes / doesn't like; food and hobby nouns (art, music, spiders, soccer, carrots).
Board: Mia = she → likes art / doesn't like spiders • Ben = he → likes soccer / doesn't like carrots
Exit Ticket — Quick Check
  • 1. True or False: Mia likes spiders. → False
  • 2. Answer: What does Ben like? → He likes soccer, dogs and pizza.
  • 3. Find the word: a small animal with eight legs → spider
Teacher Reflection: □ What worked well?  □ What didn't work?  □ Who needed extra support?  □ What will I change next time?

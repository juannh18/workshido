Answer Key
1. Write Complete Sentences
  • 1. An elephant is bigger than a mouse.
  • 2. A car is faster than a bike.
  • 3. An apartment is smaller than a house.
  • 4. A train is longer than a bus.
  • 5. A book is more interesting than a comic. (opinion – either direction accepted)
  • 6. A laptop is more expensive than a phone.
2. Complete and Personalize
1. bigger than…
4. better than…
2. easier than…
5. farther / further than…
3. taller than…
6. quieter than…

The second half of each sentence is the student's own idea.

3. Compare the Pairs

True sentences vary; models: 1 Coffee is hotter than tea. 2 Winter is colder than summer. 3 A dog is friendlier than a cat. 4 A car is more expensive than a bike. 5 A sofa is more comfortable than a chair. 6 (opinion) A movie is more interesting than a book. Check -er + than for short adjectives, more + adjective + than for long ones.

4. Correct and Rewrite
  • 1. My house is bigger than yours.
  • 2. She is taller than me.
  • 3. This book is more interesting than that one.
  • 4. A car is cheaper than a bike.
  • 5. My brother is funnier than me.
5. Look and Write Two Sentences

Personal answers – two comparative sentences per picture set (buildings, phones, jackets) using the box adjectives (tall, short, old, new, cheap, expensive) + than.

6. Write a Short Paragraph

Personal answers (4–5 sentences) comparing two things (two animals / cities / subjects / friends / hobbies). Every comparison needs 'than'.

7. Your Turn

Personal answers – 4 true comparative sentences about the student's life. Check -er / more and 'than'.

Challenge

One sentence with 'better' and one with 'worse'. e.g. My phone is better than my old one. The weather today is worse than yesterday.

Teaching Support
Teacher Tip
The Remember box splits adjectives into three types: short (add -er), long (use more), irregular (better / worse). Have students label each adjective S / L / IRR before they write – that one step prevents most errors.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ My house is more big than yours.
✓ My house is bigger than yours.
✗ She is taller that me.
✓ She is taller than me.
✗ This book is interestinger than that one.
✓ This book is more interesting than that one.
✗ A car is cheap than a bike.
✓ A car is cheaper than a bike.
Extra Class Activity
Two Things Comparison (8 min)
Hold up or name two objects (a pen and a pencil, a car and a bike). Students race to write one true comparative sentence with 'than'. Read a few aloud and check the -er / more choice; then students choose their own pairs.

Comparative Adjectives – Writing • A1
Learning Focus
Objective: Students will be able to write true comparative sentences comparing two people, places or things, using -er than for short adjectives, more … than for long ones, and better / worse.
Success Criteria: I can add -er + than to short adjectives (big → bigger than). I can use more + adjective + than for long ones. I can use better and worse. I never forget 'than'.
Prior Knowledge: Common adjectives; the spelling of -er endings (big → bigger, happy → happier); 'than' as the comparison word.
Lesson Plan (40–45 minutes)
Level: A1 Skill: Writing Topic: Writing comparative sentences (-er than / more … than) Exercises: 7 (1–7) Est. Time: 40–45 minutes
Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Pens or pencils Board for short / long / irregular adjectives
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpDraw an elephant and a mouse on the board. Ask 'Which is big?' then push for the full form: 'An elephant is bigger than a mouse.' Underline 'bigger' and 'than'.
11 minPresentation (I Do)Board three adjective types. Short (1 syllable): add -er – small→smaller, fast→faster; double the last letter after one vowel (big→bigger); y→ier (happy→happier). Long (2+ syllables): use more – more expensive, more interesting (never 'expensiver'). Irregular: good→better, bad→worse. State the fixed rule: always put than after the comparative. Model Exercise 1 item 1: 'elephant / mouse (big) → An elephant is bigger than a mouse.' Then a long one: 'phone / laptop (expensive) → A laptop is more expensive than a phone.' Model Exercise 4 item 1, showing the two errors: 'more big' should be 'bigger', and it already has 'than'.
10 minGuided Practice (We Do)Class does Exercise 1 items 1–3 together, labelling each adjective short / long first. Do Exercise 4 item 2 as a class (spot the mistake: 'that' should be 'than').
10 minIndependent Practice (You Do)Students complete Exercises 1, 3 and 4 alone, then compare Exercise 4 in pairs.
6 minAssessmentReview Exercise 4 on the board: for each, a student names the mistake and gives the correct sentence. Students complete the Exit Ticket individually.
5 minClosureStudents write two sentences from Exercise 7 about their own life and read one to a partner, who checks -er / more and 'than'.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Homework: write 5 true comparative sentences about your home, your school and your town, using at least one long adjective and one irregular (better / worse).
Anticipated Problems & Solutions
Problem: Students say 'more big' / 'more fast' or 'expensiver' / 'interestinger', mixing the two patterns.
Solution: Keep the short / long board up; students say the syllable count aloud before choosing -er or more.
Problem: Students drop 'than' or write 'that' ('She is taller me' / 'taller that me').
Solution: Write a big 'than' on the board; every comparative sentence must have it. Drill the sound /ðæn/ vs 'that'.
For Extra Support Give weaker students a short list of adjectives already sorted into short (add -er) / long (use more) / irregular to use as a model during Exercises 1 and 3.
For an Extra Challenge Students rewrite three of their sentences the other way round (swap the two things and keep the meaning true).
Language Focus & Board Notes
Grammar: short adj + -er + than; more + long adj + than; good→better, bad→worse.
Board: An elephant is bigger than a mouse. • A laptop is more expensive than a phone. • Weekends are better than Mondays.
Exit Ticket — Quick Check
  • 1. Write: a car / a bike (fast) → A car is faster than a bike.
  • 2. Fix: This book is interestinger than that one. → more interesting than
  • 3. Fix: She is taller that me. → taller than me

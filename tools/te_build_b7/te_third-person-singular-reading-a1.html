Answer Key
A. Read the Text

Text: Emma and Leo. No written answers – students read and underline third person singular verbs (gets up, has, goes, likes, reads, plays, practices, doesn't like, helps, keeps).

B. Answer the Questions
  • 1. She gets up at 7 o'clock.
  • 2. She goes to school by bus.
  • 3. She reads books (and plays the piano) in the evening.
  • 4. He doesn't like reading.
  • 5. He goes to school by bike.
  • 6. They go to the park, ride their bikes and spend time with their friends.
C. True or False
1. F (7 o'clock)
4. F (he doesn't like reading)
2. T
5. T
3. F (the piano)
6. T
D. Find the Expressions

Answers vary – any five phrases copied from the text, e.g. gets up at 7 o'clock • has breakfast with her family • goes to school by bus • reads books in the evening • helps his dad in the garden.

E. Choose the Correct Option
1. likes
4. watches
2. plays
5. helps
3. doesn't
6. go
F. Vocabulary in Context (match)
  • 1. organized → c) keep a room tidy
  • 2. energetic → b) full of energy
  • 3. practice → d) do an activity many times to get better
  • 4. pet → a) a pet animal
  • 5. close → e) very good and supportive
G. Your Turn / Think About It

Personal answers. Students write what they like / don't like in full sentences (I like… / I don't like…).

Teaching Support
Teacher Tip
This text is a controlled input flood of third person singular verbs. Before Exercise B, have students read paragraph 1 again and highlight every verb that ends in -s or -es. They will use those exact forms to answer in full sentences.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ Emma get up at seven.
✓ Emma gets up at seven.
✗ Leo don't like reading.
✓ Leo doesn't like reading.
✗ She go to school by bus.
✓ She goes to school by bus.
✗ He watch sports videos.
✓ He watches sports videos.
Extra Class Activity
Emma or Leo? (8 min)
Read out sentences from or about the text ('She plays the piano.', 'He goes to school by bike.', 'She has a cat.'). Students call out 'Emma!' or 'Leo!'. Then students make three of their own Emma/Leo sentences for a partner to identify.

Third Person Singular – Reading • A1
Learning Focus
Objective: Students will be able to read a short descriptive text and answer comprehension questions in full sentences, using third person singular verbs accurately.
Success Criteria: I can understand a short text about two people. I can answer questions with 'He / She' + a verb with -s / -es. I can find information in the text to say if a sentence is true or false.
Prior Knowledge: Present simple third person singular verb forms; daily-routine and free-time vocabulary (get up, go to school, play, read, watch).
Lesson Plan (40–45 minutes)
Level: A1 Skill: Reading Topic: Present simple third person singular in a short text (Emma and Leo) Exercises: 7 (A–G) Est. Time: 40–45 minutes
Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Highlighters or coloured pencils Pens
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpAsk the class: 'What time do you get up? How do you come to school?' Then say 'Today we read about Emma and Leo. She gets up early. He goes to school by bike.' Write those two sentences on the board and underline -s / -es.
8 minPresentation (I Do)Read paragraph 1 aloud while students follow. Stop at each third person verb (gets up, has, goes, likes, reads, plays, practices, doesn't like, helps, keeps) and think aloud: 'The subject is Emma = she, so the verb has -s.' Show how question 1 in Exercise B is answered by finding 'She gets up at 7 o'clock' in the text and writing it as a full sentence.
10 minGuided Practice (We Do)Class does Exercise B questions 1–3 together, locating the line in the text first, then writing the full-sentence answer. Students finish B alone and check in pairs.
10 minIndependent Practice (You Do)Students complete Exercises C, D and E alone.
6 minAssessmentReview Exercise E as a class, asking for the subject before the answer. Students complete Exercise F (matching) and the Exit Ticket individually.
5 minClosureStudents write two answers in Exercise G about themselves and read one to a partner.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Homework: write a short paragraph (4–5 sentences) about a brother, sister or friend, copying the style of the Emma / Leo text.
Anticipated Problems & Solutions
Problem: Students copy the text verb without changing it, or drop the -s when answering in their own words ('She get up at seven').
Solution: Insist every Exercise B answer starts with He / She / They; write the sentence frame on the board and check the verb ending before moving on.
Problem: In Exercise F (matching) students match by the first word they see rather than reading all the options.
Solution: Tell them to read all five meanings first, then start with the word they are most sure of and cross options off as they go.
For Extra Support For Exercise B, give weaker students the paragraph and question number side by side so they only have to locate and copy, not scan the whole text.
For an Extra Challenge Students write three more true/false statements about the text and swap with a partner to answer.
Extension & Fast-Finisher Ideas
Underline every verb in paragraph 2 that ends in -s. Write one true and one false sentence about Leo for a partner.
Language Focus & Board Notes
Grammar/Vocab: present simple third person singular; routine verbs (get up, go, have, play, read, watch, help).
Skills: Reading (main text + T/F + matching), Writing (full-sentence answers, Ex. G).
Board: Emma = she → gets up, goes, likes • Leo = he → plays, watches, doesn't like
Exit Ticket — Quick Check
  • 1. True or False: Emma plays the guitar. → False (the piano)
  • 2. Answer: How does Leo go to school? → He goes to school by bike.
  • 3. Choose: Emma (help / helps) her mother. → helps
Teacher Reflection: □ What worked well?  □ What didn't work?  □ Who needed extra support?  □ What will I change next time?

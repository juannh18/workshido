Teaching Support
Teacher Tip
The rule is the mirror of the comparative: short adjective → the + -est (the tallest); long adjective → the most + adjective (the most delicious); irregular → the best / the worst. The word students forget is 'the' – underline it on every board example.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ She is the taller student in the class.
✓ She is the tallest student in the class.
✗ It was most delicious pizza I ever ate.
✓ It was the most delicious pizza I ever ate.
✗ July is the most hot month.
✓ July is the hottest month.
✗ My grandma is the kindest person of the family.
✓ My grandma is the kindest person in the family.

Superlative Bingo (8 min)
Students draw a 3x3 grid and write nine adjectives. The teacher calls topics ('a river', 'a building', 'a person you know'); students write a true superlative sentence for any matching square. First to a full row reads their three sentences for a form check.

Superlative Adjectives – Practice • A1
Learning Focus
Objective: Students will be able to form and use superlative adjectives (the …-est, the most …, the best / the worst) in controlled sentences and free writing, and tell them apart from comparatives.
Success Criteria: I can make the superlative of a short adjective (tall → the tallest). I can make the superlative of a long adjective (the most expensive). I can use the best and the worst. I never forget 'the'.
Prior Knowledge: Comparative adjectives; -est / -iest spelling and doubling (hot → hottest); adjectives for size, quality and description.
Lesson Plan (40–45 minutes)
Level: A1 Skill: Practice (Grammar + Writing) Topic: Superlative adjectives: the …-est / the most …, irregular forms Exercises: 6 (1–6) Est. Time: 40–45 minutes
Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Pens or pencils Board for short / long / irregular superlatives
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpName three things: a mouse, a dog, an elephant. Ask 'Which is the biggest?' → 'The elephant is the biggest.' Write it and underline 'the' and '-est'; note we are comparing three, not two.
11 minPresentation (I Do)Board the forms next to the comparative so the pattern is visible. Short: tall → taller → the tallest; big → the biggest (double letter); happy → the happiest; easy → the easiest. Long: expensive → the most expensive; interesting → the most interesting. Irregular: good → the best, bad → the worst. State the rule: a superlative compares three or more and always starts with the. Contrast with the comparative (bigger than): the superlative has 'the' and no 'than'. Model Exercise 2 items 1, 8 and 9 aloud, saying short / long / irregular each time.
10 minGuided Practice (We Do)Class does Exercise 2 items 1–4 together, labelling short / long and watching for 'than'. Do Exercise 4 item 1 as a class (order the words, add capital and full stop).
10 minIndependent Practice (You Do)Students complete Exercises 2, 3 and 4 alone, then compare Exercise 4 in pairs.
6 minAssessmentReview Exercise 2 on the board: for each item a student gives the form and says short / long / irregular. Students complete the Exit Ticket individually.
5 minClosureStudents write two sentences from Exercise 5 and read one to a partner, who checks 'the' and the -est / most choice.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Homework: write 6 true superlative sentences about your family, your town and your country, using at least one long adjective and one irregular (the best / the worst).
Anticipated Problems & Solutions
Problem: Students write 'the most tall' / 'the beautifulest', mixing the short and long patterns.
Solution: Keep short / long on the board; students say the syllable count aloud before choosing -est or 'the most'.
Problem: Students forget 'the' before the superlative ('It is highest mountain').
Solution: Underline 'the' on every board example; a superlative sentence is not complete without it.
For Extra Support Give weaker students a comparative → superlative conversion card for 8 adjectives, so Exercise 2 becomes a conversion task.
For an Extra Challenge Students rewrite three Exercise 2 sentences as comparatives with 'than', changing the meaning to compare two things.
Language Focus & Board Notes
Grammar: the + short adj + -est; the most + long adj; the best / the worst; 'than' → comparative.
Board: the tallest building • the most expensive phone • the best season • easier than (comparative)
Exit Ticket — Quick Check
  • 1. Superlative: big → the biggest
  • 2. Superlative: interesting → the most interesting
  • 3. This exercise is the ___ (easy) I have ever done. → easiest

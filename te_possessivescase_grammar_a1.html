Answer Key
B. Practice 1 – Add 's to the Nouns

6. my brother's
2. my mom's
7. the baby's
3. the dog's
8. the cat's
4. Sarah's
9. my uncle's
5. the teacher's
10. the girls'
Item 10 is the trap: the girls is plural and ends in -s, so it takes only an apostrophe (the girls'), not 's. Point students back to the green rule box.
C. Practice 2 – Rewrite the Sentences Using 's
  • 1. This is my friend's book.
  • 2. The children's room is very clean.
  • 3. My brother's bike is red.
  • 4. That is our teacher's car.
  • 5. The dog's toys are on the floor.
Item 2: children is plural but does NOT end in -s, so it takes the full 's (children's) — a classic follow-up question after Practice 1 item 10.
D. Identify the Possessive – Noun to Underline
1. Emma's (laptop)
4. students' (classroom)
2. dog's (bone)
5. children's (books)
3. parents' (car)
6. teacher's (chair)
E. Choose the Correct Option
  • 1. This is the boy's backpack.
  • 2. The cat's food is in the kitchen.
  • 3. My sister's room is small.
  • 4. The teachers' meeting is at 3 p.m.
  • 5. The baby's bottle is over there.
⚠ Both options in each item are grammatically possible out of context. The key follows the most natural reading: one owner in items 1, 2, 3 and 5, and a group meeting in item 4 (teachers'). If a student justifies the other option (e.g. several boys sharing one backpack), accept it and discuss the difference.
F. Sentence Building – Suggested Answers
  • 1. This is my brother's guitar.
  • 2. The dogs' toys are on the floor.
  • 3. Our teacher's office is big.
  • 4. The girls' notebooks are on the desk.
  • 5. The children's playground is fun.
  • 6. My friend's birthday is today.
Answers are open — accept any correct sentence. Check the apostrophe position carefully: dogs' and girls' (plural + '), but children's (irregular plural + 's).
Teacher Tip
Teach the three cases as a simple decision tree on the board: Is the owner one person/thing? → add 's. More than one and it ends in -s? → add only '. More than one but irregular (children, people)? → add 's. Have students point at the branch they used every time they answer.

Teacher Extra Activity
Whose Desk Is This? (10 min)
Students put one personal object on their desk and stand up. Walk the class around the room: point at a desk and ask "Whose pencil is this?" — the class answers in chorus: "It's Maria's pencil!" Include pairs of students ("It's Ana and Luis's project") to practise joint possession.

Lesson Plan (45–50 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpPick up a student's pen and say "This is Ana's pen." Repeat with two or three objects. Write one sentence on the board and circle the 's — ask the class what it shows.
10 minPresentationWork through Section A (Grammar Focus): the singular table, then the green rule and the plural table. Drill the three cases on the board: Emma's / the girls' / the children's.
10 minControlled PracticeStudents complete Practice 1 (B) individually, then Practice 2 (C) in pairs. Check as a class; make students say aloud where the apostrophe goes in item 10 (girls').
10 minGuided PracticeStudents do D (identify the possessive) and E (choose the correct option). Review E by asking "one owner or many owners?" for each item before confirming the answer.
10 minProductionStudents write the six sentences of F (Sentence Building) individually. Monitor apostrophe placement. Early finishers write one extra sentence about a classmate's object.
5 minWrap-UpQuick board race: write the boy / the boys / the children and elicit the possessive form of each as fast as possible.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Students write 6 sentences about things that belong to people in their family, using at least one plural owner (my parents' car) and one irregular plural (the children's room). Next class, they read one sentence aloud and the class says where the apostrophe goes.
For Extra Support Keep the decision tree (one owner / plural -s / irregular plural) visible on the board during the whole lesson. For Practice 2, do items 1 and 2 together as models before students continue alone.
For an Extra Challenge Ask fast finishers to rewrite three sentences from E with the option they did NOT choose and explain how the meaning changes (e.g. the boy's backpack vs. the boys' backpack — one owner or several).

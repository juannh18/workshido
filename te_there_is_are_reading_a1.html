Answer Key
Exercise 1 – Before You Read (open, 5 things)
Accept any 5 reasonable items visible in the header picture (e.g., sofa, plant, bookshelf, lamp, picture, coffee table).
Exercise 2 – Read the Text: "Emma's House"
No answers needed. Key facts: living room (big sofa, coffee table, books on shelves, TV, plants) / kitchen (table, four chairs, dishes in cupboard, fridge) / two bedrooms (bed, desk, lamp in each, no TVs, pictures on walls) / bathroom (shower, sink, toilet, towels).
Exercise 3 – Reading Comprehension
  • 1. How many rooms are there in Emma's house? — There are five rooms (living room, kitchen, two bedrooms, bathroom).
  • 2. What is there in front of the sofa? — There is a coffee table.
  • 3. How many chairs are there in the kitchen? — There are four chairs.
  • 4. Is there a TV in the living room? — Yes, there is.
  • 5. What is there in each bedroom? — There is a bed, a desk and a lamp.
  • 6. Are there any TVs in the bedrooms? — No, there aren't.
  • 7. What is there in the bathroom? — There is a shower, a sink and a toilet.
  • 8. Are there any towels in the bathroom? — Yes, there are.
Exercise 4 – True or False
StatementTF
1. There is a small sofa in the living room.F
2. There are some books on the shelves.T
3. There are five chairs in the kitchen.F
4. There are three bedrooms in Emma's house.F
5. There is a TV in one of the bedrooms.F
6. There is a bath in the bathroom.F
Item 1: the text says a "big" sofa, not small. Item 3: there are four chairs, not five. Item 6: the text mentions a shower, not a bath.
Exercise 5 – Find in the Text
  • 1. a large piece of furniture to sit on → sofa
  • 2. a place where you cook food → kitchen
  • 3. a room with a bed to sleep in → bedroom
  • 4. a piece of furniture with shelves for books → shelves
  • 5. a place to wash your body → shower
Exercise 6 – Inference (open)
Accept any reasonable, justified answer. Suggested models:
1. Where does Emma like to read? → Probably in the living room, because there is a sofa and it sounds cozy.
2. Does Emma have a nice house? → Yes, because it has many rooms and comfortable furniture.
3. Personal — accept any room with a justified reason.
Teacher Tip
Before reading, ask students to predict what rooms a house usually has — this activates vocabulary for Ex 1. For Ex 4 (True/False), require students to underline the line in the text that proves their answer; this builds evidence-based reading habits. For Ex 5, model the strategy of scanning for definitions instead of reading the whole text again.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ There is four chairs.
✓ There are four chairs.
✗ There are a bed in the bedroom.
✓ There is a bed in the bedroom.
✗ There is TVs in the bedrooms.
✓ There aren't any TVs in the bedrooms.
✗ How many rooms there are?
✓ How many rooms are there?
Teacher Extra Activity
Draw Emma's House (10–15 min)
Students draw a simple floor plan of Emma's house based only on the text, labeling each room and its furniture. Pairs compare drawings and check for missing details — a fun way to verify close reading without re-answering questions directly.

Lesson Plan (45 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpShow the header picture and ask "What rooms do you have in your house?" Brainstorm room vocabulary on the board.
5 minPre-ReadingStudents complete Ex 1 individually, listing 5 things they can see, then share with a partner.
10 minWhile-ReadingStudents read "Emma's House" silently (Ex 2), then read again aloud in pairs, taking turns by paragraph.
10 minComprehensionStudents answer Ex 3 individually. Check answers as a class, asking students to point to the line in the text that supports each answer.
8 minTrue/False & VocabularyPairs complete Ex 4 and Ex 5 together, discussing disagreements before checking with the teacher.
7 minInference & Wrap-UpWhole-class discussion of Ex 6 inference questions. Close with one volunteer describing their own house using "There is / There are."

Answer Key
Model Dialogue (Sample Answer)

Server: Welcome! What would you like today?
Customer: I'd like the pasta, please.
Server: Would you like something to drink?
Customer: Yes, please. I'd like an orange juice.
Server: Would you like anything else?
Customer: I like spicy food, so I'd like some hot sauce too.
Server: Would you like dessert?
Customer: No, thanks. Just the bill, please.
Server: Here you are. Enjoy your meal!
Customer: Thank you!

Accept any 8–10 line dialogue that uses like for preferences and would like for at least two requests/offers, with correct capitalization and punctuation.

Teaching Support
Teacher Tip
Have students complete the Plan Your Order ticket (Activity 3) fully before writing a single line of dialogue — students who skip planning tend to run out of ideas after 3–4 lines.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ I like a coffee, please.
✓ I'd like a coffee, please.
✗ Would you like ordering now?
✓ Would you like to order now?
Extra Class Activity
Dialogue Swap & Perform (10 min)

Pairs exchange finished dialogues with another pair and perform each other's version aloud, checking that every line makes sense and uses like/would like correctly.

Lesson Plan (40–45 minutes)
Level: A2 Skill: Writing Topic: Like / Would like — a guided restaurant dialogue Exercises: 4 (1–4), guided writing task Est. Time: 40–45 minutes
Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Pencils or pens
TimeStageProcedure
6 minWarm-UpReview the Useful Expressions box (1) in pairs, practicing customer/server lines aloud.
6 minRead the Model (2)Students read the sample dialogue and identify each like/would like use.
8 minPlan Your Order (3)Students complete the order ticket with their own main dish, drink, dessert and preference.
15 minWrite Your Dialogue (4)Students write 8–10 lines of original dialogue using their planned order.
6 minCheck Your WritingStudents self-check against the 5-item checklist, then swap with a partner.
4 minWrap-UpA few pairs perform their dialogue for the class.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea

Homework: rewrite the dialogue as a short narrative paragraph describing what the customer ordered and why.

For Extra Support

Give students the Read the Model dialogue (2) as a direct template — they substitute only the food/drink words to produce their first dialogue, then attempt an original one.

For an Extra Challenge

Students add a complication to the dialogue (e.g., the restaurant doesn't have an item) and write the customer's polite response using would like.

Extension & Fast-Finisher Ideas
Add one more customer request using would like. Rewrite one line to make the customer more polite. Perform your dialogue changing the food to breakfast items. Write one sentence about your own food preferences using like.
Key Language & Board Notes
Target: like (general preference) vs. would like (request/offer) in a service dialogue.
Board: What would you like? I'd like the pasta, please. I like spicy food.
Exit Ticket — Quick Check
  • 1. Write one request line a customer could say.
  • 2. Write one offer line a server could say.
  • 3. Write one preference sentence with like.

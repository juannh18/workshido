Answer Key
Exercise 1 – Look and Choose (circle the correct preposition)
1. The book is in the box.
2. The cat is under the chair.
3. The ball is between the boxes.
4. The plant is next to the window.
5. The bag is in front of the desk.
6. The lamp is on the table.
Items 4 and 5 have no picture reference — both options can be grammatically logical. Accept a student's answer if they can justify it (e.g. "on the desk" for item 5 is also acceptable).
Exercise 2 – Complete the Sentences
1. The book is in the box.
2. The cat is under the chair.
3. The ball is between the two boxes.
4. The backpack is under the table.
5. The plant is on the cabinet.
6. The car is in front of the box.
This exercise has no picture, so several prepositions can be correct for items 4–5 (e.g. "next to" the table, "next to" the cabinet). Accept any answer that makes grammatical sense.
Exercise 3 – Look at the Picture and Write (open, suggested answers)
1. The backpack is next to the chair.
2. The soccer ball is in the box.
3. The trophy is on the bookshelf.
4. The plant is next to the window.
Exercise 4 – Circle the Correct Answer
1. The book is on the shelf.
2. The cat is under the table.
3. The lamp is next to the bed.
4. The ball is in front of the chair.
5. The plant is next to the books.
Item 3: "between" is not grammatically complete on its own (it needs two objects), so "next to" is the only valid option. Items 4 and 5 have no picture — accept the other option if the student explains it logically.
Exercise 5 – Write About Your Classroom (open, suggested answers)
1. The teacher's desk is in front of the board.
2. My backpack is under my chair.
3. The clock is on the wall.
4. My pencil case is next to my book.
Teacher Tip
Exercises 1, 2 and 4 don't include a picture for every item, so more than one preposition can be logically correct. Focus on whether the student's sentence is grammatically well-formed and makes sense, not on matching a single "correct" word.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ The book is in the desk.
✓ The book is on the desk.
✗ The cat is behind under the chair.
✓ The cat is under the chair.
✗ The ball is between the box.
✓ The ball is between the boxes. (needs two objects)
✗ The plant is in front the window.
✓ The plant is in front of the window.
Teacher Extra Activity
Classroom Treasure Hunt (10 min)
Call out sentences like "Find something that is under a chair" or "Find something that is next to the door." Students point to or name the object. Then have pairs take turns giving each other similar clues using the prepositions from the Grammar Box.

Lesson Plan (45 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpPlace a real object (a pen) in, on, under and next to a book on the desk while saying each sentence aloud. Ask students to repeat.
10 minPresentationGo through the Grammar Box and Visual Reference on the worksheet together, checking pronunciation of each preposition.
10 minControlled PracticeStudents complete Exercise 1 individually, then check answers in pairs, explaining their choice for items 4–5.
8 minGuided PracticeStudents complete Exercise 2 in pairs, comparing their prepositions and discussing alternatives.
8 minProductionStudents complete Exercises 3 and 4 using the room picture, then compare Exercise 3 sentences with a partner.
4 minWrap-UpStudents complete Exercise 5 about their own classroom and share one sentence with the class.

⚠ The box has 6 numbered blanks but the paragraph contains only 4 irregular plurals (sheep, oxen, people, geese). Grandparents and stories are regular and should NOT be listed. Tell students in advance that some blanks may stay empty — it turns the task into a discrimination exercise instead of a counting one.
Challenge – Suggested Answers (open)
  • E.g. "Two mice live in our garden. My baby brother has three teeth. Many people visit this park. The children play with the geese. My feet hurt after soccer practice."
Open answers — require 5 original sentences, each with a different irregular plural from this worksheet.
Teacher Tip
Group the irregulars by pattern before drilling: vowel change (man/men, foot/feet, tooth/teeth, goose/geese, mouse/mice, louse/lice), no change (sheep, fish, deer, salmon, aircraft), and special forms (children, people, oxen). Three small families are far easier to remember than one list of fifteen words.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ We saw three sheeps on the farm.
✓ We saw three sheep on the farm.
✗ The childrens are playing outside.
✓ The children are playing outside.
✗ My foots hurt.
✓ My feet hurt.
✗ There are many persons in the mall.
✓ There are many people in the mall.
Teacher Extra Activity
Irregular Snap (10 min)
In pairs, students make quick flashcards: singular on one card, plural on another (use the worksheet's tip!). Shuffle both sets face down. Players turn two cards at a time — if they are a matching pair (mouse + mice), they keep it, but only if they can say a sentence with the plural. Most pairs wins.

Lesson Plan (45–50 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpWrite one child → two ______ on the board and let the class guess. Collect other irregulars they already know. Establish the idea: some plurals break the rules.
12 minPresentationStudy the table in A together, completing the plural column. Group the words into the three families (vowel change / no change / special) on the board — see Teacher Tip. Drill pronunciation, especially women /ˈwɪmɪn/.
10 minControlled PracticeStudents complete B individually. Check as a class; celebrate whoever spots that passport is the only regular noun. Present the Latin/Greek items (data, crises, nuclei...) as curiosities, not test material.
8 minGuided PracticeStudents do C in pairs, reading the full sentences aloud. Review by asking which family each answer belongs to.
10 minApplicationStudents read the paragraph in D, underline the irregular plurals and write them in the box. Warn them first: not every blank will be used. Check and discuss why grandparents and stories don't count.
5 minWrap-Up + ChallengeIntroduce the Challenge (5 sentences with 5 different irregular plurals). Start in class, finish for homework. Exit ticket: each student says one singular→plural pair on the way out.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Students finish their flashcard set (from the extra activity) at home covering all the irregulars in this worksheet, and bring it next class for a 5-minute speed round in pairs. Bonus: find one NEW irregular plural not on the worksheet (e.g. cactus/cacti counts if they didn't reach Section B item 4 in class).
For Extra Support Limit Section B to items 1–7 (the no-change family plus passports) for weaker students, and let them keep the completed table of A visible during C and D.
For an Extra Challenge Fast finishers research the singular of data and media, and write one sentence with a Latin/Greek plural used correctly. Ask them to explain to the class why "the data are" and "the data is" both appear in modern English.

Answer Key
A. Complete the Chart – Your Turn
  • 1. car → cars
  • 2. box → boxes
  • 3. baby → babies
  • 4. key → keys
  • 5. person → people
B. Write the Plural Form
1. pen → pens
6. family → families
2. watch → watches
7. story → stories
3. party → parties
8. baby → babies
4. dress → dresses
9. city → cities
5. class → classes
10. leaf → leaves
Item 10 (leaf → leaves) uses the f → ves change, which is not in the chart of Section A. Present it as a bonus rule when checking — most students will write leafs.
C. Choose the Correct Option
  • 1. I have two books on my desk.
  • 2. My sister has three dresses.
  • 3. There are many apples in the basket.
  • 4. We saw some fish at the aquarium.
  • 5. The children are playing in the park.
  • 6. He buys new shoes every month.
Item 4: fish keeps the same form in the plural. Fishes exists in English but only for different species — at A1, teach fish as the only plural.
D. Nouns in the Paragraph
  • Singular nouns (5): weekend, family, town, park, restaurant
  • Plural nouns (6): houses, gardens, people, children, adults, meals
⚠ There are 5 singular and 6 plural nouns but only 4 printed lines per column — tell students to write two nouns per line where needed. People and children are the hidden irregulars: no final -s, but plural.
Challenge – Suggested Answers (open)
  • E.g. "There are twenty desks and twenty chairs in my classroom. My classmates keep their books in their backpacks. The windows are big and the walls have many posters."
Open answers — require 5 sentences with at least 3 plural nouns each. Have students underline their plurals for quick marking.
Teacher Tip
The worksheet's own tip says to say plurals out loud — build on it: drill the three end-sounds /s/ (books), /z/ (cars) and /ɪz/ (buses) with a quick listen-and-repeat before Section B. Students who can hear the difference stop writing watchs and dresss.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ Two babys are sleeping.
✓ Two babies are sleeping.
✗ I have three watchs.
✓ I have three watches.
✗ The childrens are in the park.
✓ The children are in the park.
✗ Many peoples live in cities.
✓ Many people live in cities.
Teacher Extra Activity
Plural Corners (10 min)
Label three corners of the room: -s, -es, -ies / irregular. Call out a singular noun from the worksheet (bus, party, child, key...). Students walk to the corner of the rule that applies and shout the plural. Wrong corner sits one round out.

Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Board and markers Three corner labels for the extra activity (-s / -es / -ies & irregular) Timer or clock for pacing
Lesson Plan (45–50 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpHold up one pen, then two: "one pen, two...?" Repeat with visible classroom objects. Write the pairs on the board and ask what changed in each word.
12 minPresentationWork through the chart in Section A row by row, eliciting the rule before reading it. Students complete the "Your Turn" column. Drill the three plural end-sounds /s/, /z/, /ɪz/ (see Teacher Tip).
10 minControlled PracticeStudents complete Section B individually. Check as a class: for each answer, students name the rule used (add -s, add -es, y → ies, or bonus f → ves in item 10).
8 minGuided PracticeStudents do Section C in pairs, reading each full sentence aloud before choosing. Review, highlighting fish (no change) and children (irregular).
10 minApplicationStudents read the paragraph in D, underline the nouns and sort them into the two columns. Check by drawing the columns on the board and collecting one noun per student.
5 minWrap-Up + ChallengeIntroduce the Challenge (5 sentences about the classroom, 3+ plurals each). Start it in class; finish as homework if time runs out.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Scavenger hunt at home: students find and write down 10 things there is more than one of in their house (4 chairs, 2 TVs, many spoons...). At least two items must use -es or -ies. Next class, compare lists in pairs and build a class "top 5 most common plurals at home".
For Extra Support Let weaker students keep the Section A chart visible and do only items 1–6 of Section B before comparing with a partner. In Section D, tell them how many nouns to find (5 singular, 6 plural) so the search is bounded.
For an Extra Challenge Fast finishers write the plural end-sound (/s/, /z/ or /ɪz/) next to each answer in Section B, then find one more noun of their own for each of the five rules in the chart.

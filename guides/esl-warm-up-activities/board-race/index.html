Board Race: ESL Warm-Up Activity

How to run Board Race as an ESL warm-up: step-by-step instructions, level variations from A1 to B2, and free related worksheets.

Time
5 min
Level
A1-B2
Grouping
Two teams
Materials
Board, 2 markers
💾 Download this activity as a PDF

Split the class into two teams lined up at the board. Call out a prompt — a translated word, a scrambled sentence, a grammar transformation — and the first student to run up and write the correct answer wins a point. It works as a warm-up for almost any grammar point you're about to teach or review.

What it practices

Board Race is a format, not a fixed grammar point — the prompts decide what it reviews:

  • Vocabulary translation or spelling fast recall under light competitive pressure, good for any level.
  • Grammar transformations ("Make this negative", "Change to past simple") — ideal review right before a grammar-focused lesson.

How to run it

  1. Split the class into two even teams lined up single-file facing the board, one marker per team.
  2. Prepare 8-10 prompts in advance matched to the grammar or vocabulary you're about to teach or review that day.
  3. Call one prompt at a time clearly and only once — repeating it removes the urgency that makes the game work.
  4. Let only the student at the front of each line run everyone else stays back until it's their turn, to avoid a scrum at the board.
  5. Award the point the instant a correct answer lands then send both front students to the back of their line.
  6. Correct wrong answers immediately for the whole class before the next prompt, so mistakes don't get reinforced by “winning.”

Classroom management tip: prep prompts that get progressively harder through the round — starting too hard kills momentum before the game builds any energy.

Level variations

  • A1Use single-word vocabulary or translation prompts, no sentence-level grammar yet.
  • A2-B1Use grammar transformations tied to the lesson (comparatives, question formation, tense changes).
  • B1-B2Use full-sentence corrections or advanced structures (modals of possibility, reported speech) for a harder, faster round.

Free worksheets for follow-up practice

Match the prompts to a full lesson on the same point:

  • Comparative Adjectives – Practice (A1) — transformation-friendly practice for a comparatives-themed round.
  • Question Words – Grammar Practice (A1) — question-formation prompts for a lower-level round.
  • Advanced Modals of Possibility – Practice (B2) — harder transformation prompts for advanced classes.

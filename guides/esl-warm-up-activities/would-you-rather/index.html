Would You Rather: ESL Warm-Up Activity

How to run Would You Rather as an ESL warm-up: step-by-step instructions, level variations from A2 to B1, and free related worksheets.

Time
5-8 min
Level
A2-B1
Grouping
Pairs
Materials
None (or question list)
💾 Download this activity as a PDF

Would You Rather asks students to choose between two options (coffee or tea? beach or mountains?) and justify their answer to a partner using “because.” It needs no materials, and the justification requirement is what makes it more than a yes/no warm-up.

What it practices

The choice itself is trivial — what it actually drills is opinion and reason language:

  • Opinion structures ("I’d rather... because...", "I prefer... to...") — core A2 functional language.
  • Comparative adjectives ("cheaper", "more relaxing") — students naturally reach for these when justifying a choice.

How to run it

  1. Prepare 5-6 question pairs in advance, ideally tied to the lesson's vocabulary (food, travel, free time).
  2. Model one yourself answer your own question with a full reason, not just the choice, so students copy the format.
  3. Require “because” in every answer a bare choice with no reason doesn't count as a turn.
  4. Run it in pairs, not whole-class so every student answers multiple questions instead of a few dominating.
  5. Swap partners after 3-4 questions so students hear different reasoning, not the same partner's opinions all warm-up.
  6. Ask 2-3 pairs to report their most interesting disagreement to the whole class to close.

Classroom management tip: write “I’d rather ___ because ___” on the board for the whole warm-up — without the visible frame, weaker students answer with one word and skip the reason.

Level variations

  • A2Keep questions concrete (food, free time, weather) and accept a simple reason clause.
  • A2-B1Require a comparative adjective in the reason (“because it's cheaper/more relaxing”).
  • B1Use abstract or hypothetical pairs (“work from home or in an office?”) that need fuller justification.

Free worksheets for follow-up practice

Build on the comparative language this warm-up naturally produces:

  • Comparative & Superlative Adjectives – Practice (A2) — controlled practice forming the comparatives students reach for here.
  • Comparative and Superlative Review (A2) — mixed review once both forms are introduced.
  • Likes & Dislikes – Speaking Practice (A1) — simpler opinion-language warm-up for lower levels.

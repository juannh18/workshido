Category Race: ESL Warm-Up Activity

How to run Category Race as an ESL warm-up: step-by-step instructions, level variations from A1 to B1, and free related worksheets.

Time
3-5 min
Level
A1-B1
Grouping
Pairs or teams
Materials
Paper or whiteboard
💾 Download this activity as a PDF

Category Race calls out a category (animals, food, jobs) and a letter; students race in pairs to write as many matching words as they can in 60 seconds. It needs zero prep and instantly reactivates vocabulary from a previous unit right before you need it.

What it practices

This game drills vocabulary recall, not grammar — pick the category to match the lesson ahead:

  • Concrete categories (animals, food, jobs, clothes) — reliable for A1, since every student already has some words.
  • Broader categories (feelings, city problems, banking terms) — needs a wider vocabulary range, better for B1.

How to run it

  1. Pick a category that matches the lesson ahead animals before an animals reading, jobs before a jobs role-play.
  2. Announce a starting letter or skip the letter constraint entirely for lower-level groups.
  3. Give exactly 60 seconds a visible timer (phone or board) keeps the energy up and the game short.
  4. Have pairs read their list aloud the rest of the class crosses out any word another pair already said.
  5. Score only surviving words whoever has the most non-duplicate words left wins the round.
  6. Run a second round with a new letter or category tied to that day's vocabulary, if time allows.

Classroom management tip: ban proper nouns and words borrowed untranslated from the students' first language before starting, or you'll spend the round arbitrating instead of racing.

Level variations

  • A1Skip the letter constraint entirely — naming words in the category (“animals”) is challenge enough on its own.
  • A2Add the letter constraint and require full nouns spelled correctly, not just remembered fragments.
  • B1Use abstract categories (feelings, city problems, banking terms) that need a wider working vocabulary.

Free worksheets for follow-up practice

Follow up with a full vocabulary set on whichever category you raced:

  • Animals – Vocabulary Practice (A1) — farm, pet and wild animal vocabulary in one set.
  • Food – Vocabulary Practice (A1) — core food vocabulary for a food-category race.
  • Jobs – Vocabulary Practice (A1) — common jobs and occupations.

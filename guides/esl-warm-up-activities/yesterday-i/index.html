“Yesterday I...”: ESL Warm-Up Activity

How to run the “Yesterday I...” memory warm-up: step-by-step instructions, level variations from A1 to A2, and free related worksheets.

Time
5-8 min
Level
A1-A2
Grouping
Whole class / circle
Materials
None
💾 Download this activity as a PDF

Students go around a circle completing “Yesterday I ___” with a real activity, and each new student has to recall and repeat everyone before them before adding their own line. It's a memory-game format that drills past simple naturally, with the challenge increasing every turn.

What it practices

The recall pressure is what makes this different from a simple past-simple drill:

  • Past simple form ("I watched", "I went", "I ate") — mixes regular and irregular verbs by design, since real days rarely use only one type.
  • Third-person recall ("She watched... He went...") — the repeating student has to switch pronouns correctly, not just remember content.

How to run it

  1. Model the first line yourself “Yesterday I ___” with a real, simple activity — keep your own example short.
  2. Have the next student repeat your line in third person, then add their own “Yesterday you/she watched TV, and yesterday I cooked dinner.”
  3. Keep going around the circle each student repeating everyone before them, oldest line first.
  4. Allow classmates to help with recall quietly, if someone blanks — the goal is fluency practice, not memory-test anxiety.
  5. Cap the circle at 8-10 students before starting a new round, so the recall chain doesn't collapse under its own length.
  6. Close with the last student retelling the entire class's activities in one go, if the group is confident enough.

Classroom management tip: seat students in a visible circle or U-shape for this one — recall is much harder when students can't see who said what, and eye contact naturally cues turn-taking.

Level variations

  • A1Supply a small verb bank on the board (watched, played, cooked, studied) so students aren't inventing vocabulary under memory pressure.
  • A1-A2Require one irregular verb per turn (went, ate, saw) once regular -ed forms are solid.
  • A2Add a time detail to each line (“Yesterday at 7pm I...”) for extra recall difficulty and richer sentences.

Answer Key
Exercise 1 – Sentence Builder
Example given: I / book / interesting → My book is interesting.
  • 1. you / backpack / new → Your backpack is new.
  • 2. he / bike / fast → His bike is fast.
  • 3. she / room / tidy → Her room is tidy.
  • 4. it / tail / long → Its tail is long.
  • 5. we / classroom / big → Our classroom is big.
  • 6. they / friends / fun → Their friends are fun.
Item 6: watch for the plural — are, not is.

Exercise 3 – Independent Writing (Model Answer)
Hi! My name is Ana and this is my family. Our house is small but nice. My brother loves his video games and my sister plays with her dolls. We have a cat — its fur is very soft. Our parents work a lot, but their weekends are for us. I love my family!
Check against the worksheet's own checklist: at least six possessive adjectives, clear sentences, capital letters and punctuation, spelling.
Teacher Tip
In Exercise 1, students only get the subject pronoun — the transformation pronoun → possessive adjective is the whole point. Before they start, drill the seven pairs orally (I→my, you→your...) and leave the word bank from Exercise 2 visible on the board.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ You backpack is new.
✓ Your backpack is new.
✗ Their friends is fun.
✓ Their friends are fun.
✗ It's tail is very fluffy.
✓ Its tail is very fluffy.
✗ Her favorite subject is Art (for Emma's friend Tom).
✓ Match the possessive to the person in the picture.
Teacher Extra Activity
Picture Dictation (10 min)
Describe an imaginary family photo aloud ("This is my mother. Her hair is long. Our dog is next to her...") while students draw what they hear. Then students compare drawings in pairs and retell the description using the correct possessive adjectives.

Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Board and markers Blank paper for the picture dictation activity Timer or clock for pacing
Lesson Plan (45–50 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpWrite "I → my" on the board and elicit the remaining six pairs from the class. Leave the complete chart visible for the whole lesson.
8 minPresentationReview the Writing Tip box together: possessive adjectives come before a noun. Analyze the example "I / book / interesting → My book is interesting" and identify the transformation step by step.
10 minControlled PracticeStudents complete Exercise 1 (Sentence Builder) individually. Check on the board, highlighting item 6 (plural: "Their friends are fun").
10 minGuided PracticeStudents complete Exercise 2 in pairs, using the pictures (Emma, Rex, Tom and friends, the Johnsons) to decide who owns what. Review answers as a class.
12 minProductionStudents write their paragraph in Exercise 3 (at least six possessive adjectives) and self-check with the Writing Checklist. Circulate and give on-the-spot corrections.
5 minWrap-UpVolunteers read their paragraph aloud; the class counts the possessive adjectives on their fingers as they listen.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Students bring (or draw) a photo of their family, pet, or room and write 5 sentences describing it with possessive adjectives ("This is our kitchen. Its walls are yellow..."). Display the work on the classroom wall next lesson.
For Extra Support For Exercise 3, give weaker students sentence starters ("My name is... / Our house is... / My brother loves his...") so they only fill the content. Accept 4 possessive adjectives instead of 6 if needed.
For an Extra Challenge Ask fast finishers to extend their paragraph to 8+ sentences using all seven possessive adjectives at least once — including its and your (e.g., addressing the reader: "What about your family?").

Answer Key
Vocabulary in Context
1. apartment → d
2. bookcase → c
3. bicycle → e
4. desk → a
5. dog → b
Comprehension
1. What has Leo got in his apartment? → A comfortable bed, a desk for his computer, and a big bookcase.
2. How does Leo go to university? → By bicycle. (He uses his bike.)
3. What is his dog's name? → Max.
4. Has Leo got a car? → No, he hasn't.
5. What does Leo prefer to use? Why? → He prefers his bike, because it's fast and healthy (and he doesn't mind not having a car).
Use It! – Complete with have got / has got
1. I have got a new backpack.
2. My sister has got a laptop.
3. We have got a big garden.
4. He has got two brothers.
5. She has got a beautiful cat.
6. They have got English class on Mondays.
Speaking (open, pair work)
No fixed answer key — students ask and answer "Have you got...?" questions about themselves using the short answers modelled on the worksheet ("Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.").
Real Life Task (open)
No fixed answer key — students write 4–5 sentences about their own belongings using have got / has got, following Leo's text as a model.
Teacher Tip
Before Comprehension, ask students to underline every "have got / has got / haven't got" form in the text — this doubles as a quick grammar review and makes locating answers to the "wh-" questions much faster.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ Leo have got a dog.
✓ Leo has got a dog. (he/she/it → has got)
✗ Has Leo got a car? Yes, he got.
✓ Has Leo got a car? No, he hasn't.
✗ He go to university by bicycle.
✓ He goes to university by bicycle.
✗ I haven't got no problem.
✓ I haven't got any problem. (any, not no, after haven't)
Teacher Extra Activity
My Life, My Things (10–15 min)
Using Leo's text as a model, students write 4–5 sentences about their own room or belongings, then read them to a partner, who draws what they hear without seeing the original sentences.

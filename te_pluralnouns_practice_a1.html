Answer Key
A. Write the Plural Form
1. book → books
7. shelf → shelves
2. baby → babies
8. hero → heroes
3. city → cities
9. potato → potatoes
4. bus → buses
10. knife → knives
5. brush → brushes
11. child → children
6. lady → ladies
12. goose → geese
This section mixes every rule: -s, -ies, -es, o → oes (heroes, potatoes), f/fe → ves (shelves, knives) and irregulars (children, geese). Check rule by rule, not item by item.
B. Choose the Correct Plural Form
1. two boxes
5. The children
2. My teeth
6. those geese
3. some fish
7. new shoes
4. three dresses
8. The men
C. Sort the Nouns into the Correct Column
  • Regular (add -s), 2: dogs, chairs
  • Regular (add -es), 3: buses, boxes, tomatoes
  • Irregular (change form), 11: sheep, children, mice, women, leaves, teeth, cities, men, feet, oxen, geese
⚠ Under this worksheet's three labels, cities (y → ies) and leaves (f → ves) belong in "change form" because the word changes its spelling — but many coursebooks group them with the regulars. Accept either placement if the student explains the rule. Note the columns have 4 lines each, so students must write 2–3 words per line in the irregular column.
D. Rewrite the Sentences in the Plural
  • 1. There are (two/some) cats under the table.
  • 2. She has new dresses for the party.
  • 3. My brother lost his teeth yesterday.
  • 4. We saw (some) mice in the kitchen.
  • 5. The men gave us some good advice.
Item 1 also needs is → are; remind students to check the verb, not only the noun. In items 1 and 4, accept any correct quantifier (two, some, many) or none.
E. Complete the Sentences
  • 1. five children   2. two knives   3. three big cities   4. many leaves   5. The geese
F. Plural Nouns in the Paragraph
  • Regular (8): cows, horses, tools, stories, goats, photos, animals, fields
  • Irregular (3): sheep, children, geese
⚠ The instruction says "underline the nouns", but the two columns only fit PLURAL nouns — tell students to underline plural nouns only. Singulars like weekend, family, farm, farmer, sister don't belong in either column.
Challenge – Suggested Answers (open)
  • E.g. "My classroom has twenty desks and twenty-five chairs. The children put their books on the shelves. Two women teach us English and math."
Teacher Tip
This is a review worksheet — resist re-teaching. Run Section A as a diagnostic first: check it immediately and tally on the board which rules the class missed (-es? f → ves? irregulars?). Then spend your energy only on the two weakest rules before continuing with B–F.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ Two knifes are on the table.
✓ Two knives are on the table.
✗ I like potatos.
✓ I like potatoes.
✗ There are cats under the table. (verb left singular: "There is cats")
✓ There ARE cats under the table.
✗ The mans are waiting for the bus.
✓ The men are waiting for the bus.
Teacher Extra Activity
Plural Auction (12 min)
Write 10 sentences on the board, some correct and some with plural errors ("The childs are happy"). Teams get 100 points to "buy" only the correct sentences. Highest-value correct portfolio wins. Errors bought cost double. Forces careful re-reading of every plural.

Lesson Plan (45–55 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpRapid-fire recall: say a singular noun from previous lessons (baby, box, child, foot...) and the class shouts the plural. Note the hesitations — they tell you where today's focus is.
10 minDiagnosticStudents complete Section A alone, no help. Check immediately and tally missed rules on the board (see Teacher Tip). Briefly re-explain only the two weakest rules.
8 minControlled PracticeStudents do B individually and C in pairs. When checking C, ask each pair to justify one placement with the rule ("boxes: ends in -x, add -es").
10 minSentence LevelStudents rewrite the sentences in D. Insist on the double change in item 1: noun AND verb (There are cats). Then complete E individually as a quick self-test.
10 minApplicationStudents read the paragraph in F, underline the plural nouns and sort them. Check on the board; discuss why sheep, children, geese go in the irregular column.
7 minWrap-Up + ChallengeDo the Challenge (5 sentences about the classroom, 3+ plurals each) as a mini writing sprint. Volunteers read one sentence; the class counts the plurals on their fingers.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Error-hunt homework: give students a short paragraph you write with 6 hidden plural mistakes (childs, potatos, knifes, mans, sheeps, citys). They must find and correct all six. Next class, pairs compare corrections before the class check — it mirrors the auction game at sentence level.
For Extra Support Write the six rule reminders in a corner of the board (-s / -es / -ies / -oes / -ves / irregular) and let weaker students label each answer in A and C with the rule they used. In F, tell them the totals (8 regular, 3 irregular).
For an Extra Challenge Fast finishers write two sentences from D again with a different quantifier each (a few mice, several dresses) and one original sentence combining a regular and an irregular plural.

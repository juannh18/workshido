Answer Key
A. Before Reading – Suggested Answers (open)
  • 1. Maybe she is a (high school) student. / She is Mia.
  • 2. The dog belongs to the girl. (It is Mia's dog, Rocky.)
  • 3. They are at home, in the living room.
Prediction questions — accept any reasonable answer. Come back to them after reading to confirm or correct the predictions.
Vocabulary in Context – Suggested Meanings
  • 1. amazing (adj.): very, very good; wonderful
  • 2. garden (n.): an outside area with flowers and plants next to a house
  • 3. exciting (adj.): something that makes you feel happy and interested
  • 4. photos (n.): pictures you take with a camera or phone
  • 5. wagging (v.): moving quickly from side to side (a happy dog's tail)
C. Comprehension
  • 1. She makes pancakes. (Her mom's pancakes are amazing.)
  • 2. She goes to her grandfather's house.
  • 3. The flowers in her grandfather's garden.
  • 4. They are Lily's brothers.
  • 5. She takes lots of photos with her friends.
  • 6. Rocky is Mia's dog. He is very happy — his tail never stops wagging.
D. Thinking More – Suggested Answers (open)
  • 1. Because she spends time with her family and friends — the text says her family's time is the best time.
  • 2. Open answer, e.g. "I like to watch movies with my family and visit my grandmother's house."
E. Personal Response – Model Answers (open)
  • 1. "Someone special in my family is my grandmother. I like her stories and her food. My grandmother's cakes are amazing."
  • 2. "My perfect weekend is with my friends. On Saturday we play soccer in the park. On Sunday I go to my cousin's house and we watch movies."
Encourage at least one possessive 's in each answer — that is the hidden goal of both writing tasks.
Teacher Tip
All the possessive forms in the text are printed in bold (Her mom's, Lily's, the boys', her family's). After the first reading, have students copy them into two columns — "one owner" vs. "more than one owner" — before doing the comprehension questions. It turns the bold text into a discovery grammar task.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ Mia goes to the house of her grandfather.
✓ Mia goes to her grandfather's house.
✗ The brothers of Lily are at the park.
✓ Lily's brothers are at the park.
✗ The boy's soccer game is exciting. (several boys)
✓ The boys' soccer game is exciting.
✗ Rockys tail never stops wagging.
✓ Rocky's tail never stops wagging.
Teacher Extra Activity
Whose Weekend? (10 min)
In pairs, students interview each other about their weekend with three questions (Where do you go? Who do you see? What do you eat?). Then each student reports their partner's weekend to another pair using possessives: "Carla's Saturday is at her aunt's house. Her aunt's food is amazing."

Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Board and markers Two-column chart ready on the board (one owner / many owners) Timer or clock for pacing
Lesson Plan (45–50 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpStudents look only at the photo. Do Section A (Before Reading) orally in pairs, then collect predictions on the board without confirming them yet.
10 minFirst ReadingStudents read "Mia's Weekend" silently once. Return to the board predictions: which ones were right? Quick gist questions: Who is Rocky? Who is Lily?
8 minVocabularyStudents complete Vocabulary in Context using the sentences around each word — no dictionaries. Compare guesses in pairs before checking as a class.
12 minSecond Reading + ComprehensionStudents re-read and answer Section C individually. Check as a class, asking students to read aloud the sentence in the text that contains each answer.
10 minProductionStudents answer D (Thinking More) and E (Personal Response) in writing. Require at least one possessive 's per answer. Volunteers read one answer aloud.
5 minWrap-UpBoard game: write mom / grandfather / Lily / boys / family and elicit the possessive phrase from the text for each one from memory.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Students write a short paragraph titled "My Weekend" (5–6 sentences) imitating the text, including at least three possessive forms (my mom's..., my friend's..., my parents'...). Next class, they exchange paragraphs and underline every possessive their partner used.
For Extra Support Let weaker students answer Section C with the text visible and the bold possessives already sorted in the two-column chart. Accept short answers ("pancakes") instead of full sentences.
For an Extra Challenge Fast finishers rewrite three sentences from the text replacing the possessive with an "of" structure and compare which version sounds natural (Lily's brothers → the brothers of Lily) — then explain why English prefers 's for people.

Teacher Tip
Draw a two-column chart on the board (subject pronoun → possessive adjective) and elicit each pair from students before starting. Keep it visible while they work — most A1 errors disappear when the reference chart is in sight.

Teacher Extra Activity
Whose Is It? (10 min)
Collect one small object from several students (pen, eraser, keyring). Hold each one up and ask "Whose is this?" — the class answers with a full sentence: "It's her pencil! It's his eraser!" Fast, fun, and forces his/her/their choices in real time.

Lesson Plan (45–50 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpHold up your own pen and say "This is my pen." Point to a student's bag: "This is her bag." Elicit more examples from the room without explaining the rule yet.
10 minPresentationGo through the reference table at the top of the worksheet. Build the pronoun → possessive adjective chart on the board and drill pronunciation, highlighting its vs. it's and his vs. her.
10 minControlled PracticeStudents complete Exercise 1 individually. Check as a class: read each sentence aloud and have students call out the correct option.
10 minGuided PracticeStudents complete Exercise 2 in pairs, deciding together which possessive adjective fits each subject. Review answers, asking "Who does it belong to?" for each item.
10 minProductionStudents complete Exercise 3 individually, rewriting the sentences. Early finishers write one extra sentence about a classmate's object.
5 minWrap-UpQuick oral quiz: say a subject pronoun ("they!") and students respond with the possessive adjective and an example sentence.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Students write 6 sentences about objects at home using a different possessive adjective in each one (my bedroom, our kitchen, their car...). Next class, they read one sentence aloud and the class identifies the possessive adjective used.
For Extra Support Let weaker students keep the reference table visible and complete only the odd-numbered items of Exercise 2 before comparing with a partner. For Exercise 3, do the first two items together on the board as extra models.
For an Extra Challenge Ask fast finishers to rewrite three sentences from Exercise 1 in the plural or with a different subject (e.g., "This is my laptop" → "These are our laptops"), keeping the possessive adjective consistent.

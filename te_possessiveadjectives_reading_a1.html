Answer Key
Text: "A Day in Our Lives" — Jake's blog post about a typical Saturday with his family.
Vocabulary in Context – Match the Words (1–6 with a–f)
1. typical → c. usual or normal
4. neighbors → a. people who live near you
2. famous → d. known by many people
5. company → f. the people you spend time with
3. friendly → e. kind and nice
6. wonderful → b. something very good
Exercise 1 – Answer the Questions (Suggested Answers)
  • 1. He usually helps his mom in the kitchen (and sets the table).
  • 2. She feeds their dog, Max.
  • 3. They ride their bikes in the park near their house.
  • 4. They meet their friends (who bring their soccer ball).
  • 5. She cooks their favorite lunch.
  • 6. They watch a movie in their living room (and sometimes invite their neighbors).
Exercise 2 – True or False
StatementAnswer
1. Jake helps his dad in the kitchen.F — he helps his mom
2. Max is their cat.F — Max is their dog
3. The park is near their house.T
4. Jake and his friends play soccer in the park.T
5. Jake's grandparents live far from their house.F — their house is not far
6. They watch movies in the living room.T
7. Jake thinks he has a great life.T
Exercise 3 – Think and Answer (Suggested Answers)
1. Open answer. Example: "On weekends, I visit my grandparents with my family. We have lunch at their house and play with our cousins."
2. Open answer. Example: "My neighbors are an old couple. They are very kind. We sometimes talk in their garden."
Teacher Tip
Before answering the questions, have students underline every possessive adjective in the text (there are many: my, her, his, our, their). Ask "Who does it refer to?" for a few of them — this connects the grammar focus to reading comprehension.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ Jake helps her mom.
✓ Jake helps his mom.
✗ They ride his bikes in the park.
✓ They ride their bikes in the park.
✗ Max is his dog of Jake.
✓ Max is Jake's dog. / Max is their dog.
✗ Answering T/F without correcting: "F."
✓ "F — he helps his mom." (ask for the correction)
Teacher Extra Activity
My Typical Saturday (10–15 min)
Students draw a simple 4-panel comic of their own Saturday (morning / afternoon / evening / night) and label each panel with one sentence containing a possessive adjective. Volunteers present their day to the class.

Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Board and markers Colored pencils for underlining possessives Timer or clock for pacing
Lesson Plan (45–50 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpAsk: "What do you usually do on Saturdays with your family?" Collect 3–4 answers on the board and point out any possessive adjectives students use naturally.
10 minFirst ReadingStudents read Jake's blog post silently. Then read it aloud as a class and have students underline every possessive adjective they find, checking who each one refers to.
8 minVocabularyStudents complete the Vocabulary in Context matching (1–6 with a–f) in pairs, using the text to work out the meanings. Check as a class.
10 minComprehensionStudents answer Exercise 1 in complete sentences individually. Remind them to reuse possessive adjectives from the text (his mom, their dog, their friends).
7 minTrue or FalseStudents complete Exercise 2, correcting every false statement aloud during feedback ("F — Max is their dog").
10 minProduction & Wrap-UpStudents write their answers to Exercise 3 (Think and Answer). Volunteers share their typical weekend; the class listens for possessive adjectives.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
Students write a short blog post (5–6 sentences) titled "A Day in Our Lives" about their own family, following Jake's text as a model and using at least five possessive adjectives. Collect next class for quick written feedback.
For Extra Support Allow weaker students to answer Exercise 1 with short answers first (e.g., "his mom"), then help them expand into full sentences. For the vocabulary matching, do the first item together as a model.
For an Extra Challenge Ask fast finishers to rewrite two sentences of the text changing the narrator (e.g., Jake → Lily: "My brother helps our mom in the kitchen"), adjusting every possessive adjective correctly.

Teacher Tip
Encourage students to read the text more than once. Ask them to circle key information (names, places, hobbies) before answering the questions — this helps them locate answers faster and builds active reading habits.

Materials & Prep
Printed worksheet copies (1 per student) Board and markers Timer or clock for pacing Optional: photo of a person to introduce
Lesson Plan (45 minutes)
TimeStageProcedure
5 minWarm-UpShow the photo and ask students to guess who the person is and where they are from before reading.
10 minPresentationRead the text aloud once while students follow along, then review the verb to be forms (am/is/are) on the board.
10 minControlled PracticeStudents complete Exercise 3A and 3B individually, then check answers in pairs.
8 minGuided PracticeStudents complete Exercise 4, finding every verb to be form in the text.
8 minProductionStudents complete Exercise 5 and Exercise 7, then share one sentence with a partner.
4 minWrap-UpA few volunteers introduce themselves to the class using the verb to be, following Daniel's text as a model.
Follow-Up / Homework Idea
For homework, students write a short profile of a family member or friend using the verb to be, following the same structure as Daniel's text. Collect next class for quick feedback, or have students read theirs aloud in small groups.
For Extra Support Pair lower-level students with a stronger partner for Exercise 4. Let them underline the verb to be forms in a different colour before writing them out, and allow them to copy full sentences from the text for Exercise 7 instead of writing from scratch.
For an Extra Challenge Ask faster finishers to rewrite two sentences from the text in the negative form, or to write one extra question they would ask Daniel using the verb to be.

Answer Key
Exercise 1 – Complete with have got / has got
1. I have got a new laptop.
2. She has got two sisters.
3. They have got a big garden.
4. He has got a red bicycle.
5. We have got a dog.
6. You have got an interesting book.
7. It has got a long tail.
8. My brother has got a guitar.
Exercise 2 – Choose the Correct Option
1. I have got a blue pen.
2. My parents have got a small flat.
3. He has got a new phone.
4. We have got a Geography lesson today.
5. They have got three cats.
6. She has got a green jacket.
7. You have got a lot of friends.
8. The dog has got big ears.
Exercise 3 – Make the Sentences Negative
  • 1. I haven't got a dictionary.
  • 2. She hasn't got a brother.
  • 3. They haven't got a new car.
  • 4. He hasn't got a pet.
  • 5. We haven't got English on Monday.
  • 6. You haven't got a ticket.
  • 7. It hasn't got a beautiful colour.
  • 8. My friend hasn't got a skateboard.
Exercise 4 – Write Questions
  • 1. Have you got a camera?
  • 2. Has she got any brothers?
  • 3. Have they got a garden?
  • 4. Has he got a computer?
  • 5. Have we got English today?
  • 6. Has it got big eyes?
Exercise 5 – Write Complete Sentences
  • 1. I have got a new tablet. (I've got a new tablet.)
  • 2. My family has got a holiday home.
  • 3. She has got a lot of books.
  • 4. They have got a football pitch.
  • 5. He has got a motorbike.
  • 6. We have got two English teachers.
Teacher Tip
Drill the Form Summary table chorally before the exercises: point at "I/You/We/They" and elicit "have got", then "He/She/It" and elicit "has got". This pattern (not the meaning) is what students get wrong most, so automate it first.
Common Student Mistakes
✗ She have got a brother.
✓ She has got a brother.
✗ I hasn't got a car.
✓ I haven't got a car.
✗ Has you got a pencil?
✓ Have you got a pencil?
✗ He don't have got a dog.
✓ He hasn't got a dog. (no "don't" with have got)
Teacher Extra Activity
Find Someone Who... (10 min)
Students walk around asking classmates "Have you got a...?" questions (pet, bike, brother/sister, etc.) and write down the name of a classmate who answers "Yes, I have" for each item. Wrap up by reporting back with "She has got..." / "He has got...".
